# Templar Gear and Slots

> **In one sentence:** a Season 1 Templar's finished set is the Vakron armor
> pieces plus Decurion for whatever Vakron misses, a crafted Unique Dragon
> Lord Longsword and Guard if the crafting economy allows and Enraged Kromede
> if it does not, and a permanent Belt and Amulet at Unique +10 — with the
> Guard, not the weapon, taking the first breakthroughs and the first tuning.

## Why it matters

Two facts about AION 2 equipment change how a Templar shops, and both are
covered in more detail in [[Weapons]] and [[Armor]].

**Armor is not class-restricted.** AION 2 dropped the plate / chain / leather
/ cloth split that AION 1 used. Every set contains exactly one Breastplate,
one Helm and so on, with no material variants and no class lock, so you mix
by stats and never pass on a piece because it "isn't tank armor". Do not carry
AION 1 knowledge into this. Confidence medium-high — inferred from the set
data plus one explicit English source rather than from a patch note.

**The Guard is not a shield.** The Guard (가더, sometimes transliterated
"Guarder") is a universal off-hand that all classes equip, not a tank item.
It matters enormously to a Templar anyway, for a reason specific to this
class: it is one of only two slots that can roll **Weapon Damage
Amplification** and **Combat Speed**, and it also rolls **Block** as a soul
imprint line. For a class whose damage kit runs off blocking, that makes the
Guard the single densest item in the set.

## The Season 1 grade ceiling

Best in slot here means **Unique**, not Heroic. The reported global launch
build omits Heroic-grade equipment, and every Heroic item in the Korean data
— the Ancient Spirit and Faded Shadow armor sets, the Ludra's and Lava Heart
weapon families, Ludra's Bracelet, the Heroic Belt and Amulet conversions —
depends either on Sanctuary content or on the July 2026 conversion
blueprints. Confidence on the no-Heroic claim is **medium**, single-source;
see [[Global Launch Differences]]. Where a Korean guide's answer is Heroic, the
Season 1 substitute is named below.

Two other Season 1 exclusions worth knowing before you plan:

- **No Potential** (KR 2026-03-04). Potential is a flat PvE damage layer on
  dungeon gear worth up to 2.4% weapon damage. Without it, crafted gear is
  *disproportionately* stronger than dungeon gear relative to what Korean
  guides describe.
- **No Succession / Inheritance** at launch, or only its early punitive
  version (KR Season 2, made free and guaranteed 2026-07-01). Plan on gear
  replacement being a full re-investment.

### The Decurion question, stated honestly

Korean Season 1-era gearing threads recommend heavy use of the **Decurion**
(십부장) set — the first rung of the Abyss military-rank ladder, bought with
Abyss Points at item level 67. The recommended finished Season 1 set in the
most-cited Inven gearing thread is literally "Vakron set, Decurion armor for
the slots Vakron doesn't cover, all-Decurion accessories, and either the
Decurion or the Vakron Guard".

That sits awkwardly against [[Armor]], which records that the registered Abyss
equipment sets carry a **−5% PvE Damage Boost penalty at every set tier**,
starting from 2 pieces, and concludes that Abyss gear is a dedicated PvP set.

I could not resolve this from the available sources and I am not going to
pretend otherwise. The two readings that fit the evidence:

1. The registered set bonuses belong to the **higher** Abyss ranks (the
   Guardian and Archon High Commander sets that [[Armor]] names), and the
   Decurion tier has no registered set and therefore no penalty.
2. The penalty is real on Decurion too, and Korean players accept it in the
   mid game because at item level 67 the raw stats outweigh 5% of an
   amplification pool that is still small.

**What to do:** buy Decurion accessories and armor as the Korean guides say —
they are the cheapest item level available to a mid-game character and the
Abyss Point income is otherwise idle — but **read the set tooltip in the
Abyss shop on day one** before you commit to more than a couple of pieces,
and record what you find in [[Global Launch Differences]]. If the penalty is
present at 2 pieces, Decurion becomes a fill-the-gaps set rather than a
half-your-character set, and Vakron plus market Uniques take the slack.

**The weapon call.** Crafted beats dropped in Season 1 and the margin is
larger than it looks: the White Dragon Lord tier reads item level 78 against
Bakarma's 68, carries a **fifth soul imprint line**, and carries a fixed +5%
PvE Damage Amplification that dropped weapons do not have. With Potential
absent at launch, dungeon weapons lose their compensating layer entirely.

But do not overpay for the tier. The Korean gearing thread is explicit:
**a 3-breakthrough True Dragon Lord beats a 2-breakthrough White Dragon
Lord**, so buy the tier you can afford to breakthrough three times rather
than the highest tier you can afford to own. If the crafted market is
unaffordable in your first month, Enraged Kromede at 3 breakthroughs is a
legitimate finished Season 1 weapon.

*Which specific Unique Dragon Lord tiers are craftable at Season 1 is
unresolved* — [[Weapons]] places the family in the KR Season 2–3 window while
also concluding it becomes the launch-era ceiling under a no-Heroic build.
Check the Blacksmithing recipe list on day one.

**Why the Guard goes first.** Three reasons stack:

1. It is one of only two slots that roll Weapon Damage Amplification and
   Combat Speed, the two strongest imprint lines in the game.
2. It rolls **Block** as an imprint line. On Bakarma's Guard the Block line
   appears at a 7% roll chance for 28–39 points — the highest-value single
   source of Block you can *aim* at in Season 1.
3. The Korean gearing order is unambiguous: *"Guard to 2 breakthroughs first,
   and tune every Unique item into the Guard to raise its options."* Spare
   Unique drops are Soul Binding reroll fodder, and the Guard is where they
   go. See [[Soul Imprint and Soul Binding]] and [[Extraction and Refunds]].

The Korean board's stated payoff: **weapon at 3 breakthroughs plus Guard at 2
breakthroughs, both with imprint work done, opens Transcendence stage 9.**

### The seven armor slots

The `Vakron` set is the Season 1 armor answer and the reason is efficiency,
not perfection. Vakron's Sky Island Conquest opens at item level 1,600, its
armor drop rates are much better than its weapon rates, and Korean guides
report **three or four Vakron pieces alone carrying you from 1,600 to
2,400** — the gate for Krao Hard and Fire Temple Conquest. A full set swap was
measured at about +150 item level.

**Notes on the armor plan:**

- **Do not try to complete a matched PvE set.** PvE dungeon armor sets have no
  registered set bonus. Only the Abyss sets do, and theirs is a PvP bonus with
  a PvE penalty. Mix by slot, always.
- **Enhance evenly, not deeply.** All pieces to +1, then all to +2. Below +10
  each enchant level is 1 Combat Power; above +10 it is 2, so +10 is the
  breakpoint where enhancement starts paying double. Get every piece to +3 to
  +5 during the story, standardise to +8 to +10 before pushing Hard difficulty.
- **Transitional armor stops at +5 to +7.** Rare/blue armor you are passing
  through gets low-grade manastones and light enchants; Unique armor you are
  keeping gets Intermediate manastones and real work.
- **Armor and accessory enhancement had its Defense and HP contribution
  doubled** in the KR patch of 2025-12-31, so pre-2026 guides understate it.
  Whether that doubling is in the launch build is unverified.
- **Crafted Unique Dragon Lord armor** exists under **Tailoring** and carries
  5% PvE Damage Tolerance per piece plus one extra imprint line. It is a
  market play more than a personal-power play, because armor can be farmed and
  weapons cannot. If you do craft armor, craft **Helm, Breastplate and Cloak**
  — that is the Korean three-slot compromise and it maps exactly onto the
  three slots whose imprint lines a Templar cares most about.

cheapest route to item level early. Take every drop that raises item level and
do not invest in it.

## Realistic acquisition order

This is the order I would actually follow, which is not the same as the
priority order — it is priority filtered through what is *available* when.

1. **Level 45 main-story Longsword.** Free, Unique, and the first thing worth
   enhancing. Get to +10 and stop.
2. **The Ascension Bracelet** from the level 45 Ascension quest. If the quest
   does not appear, check the Ascension gauge above the quest log.
3. **The Noble Belt scroll loop**, started on your first Stronghold clear, and
   the **Revelation Amulet** loop, started on your first Monolith donation.
   Both run in the background for the rest of the plan.
4. **Fill every empty slot** from Conquest Krao Cave and Draupnir drops and
   from the market. Item level 55 Unique armor and accessories are cheap;
   Heritage-grade item level 59 pieces from Urugugu and Vakron run roughly
   150,000 to 300,000 Kinah. Empty slots are the most expensive mistake in
   the game.
5. **Any Guard at all**, then upgrade it as opportunities appear. Do not run
   Conquest content with an empty off-hand.
6. **Decurion rings** at 31,600 AP each, the moment Abyss Points allow. Then
   earrings, cloak and gloves at 42,100. Then necklace, boots and pauldrons at
   52,600. **Chest, legs and weapon last**, at 60,000–120,000+, because by
   then you should have Vakron and crafted equivalents. Replace your worst
   pieces first and avoid buying a Decurion piece that duplicates a Unique
   you already hold.
7. **Vakron's Sky Island Conquest**, from item level 1,600, until you have the
   Guard and three to four armor pieces. Korean guides also say run it until
   the **Forest Spirit wing feather** drops, which is a separate reward worth
   staying for.
8. **Decurion armor** for whatever Vakron did not cover.
9. **Guard to 2 breakthroughs**, tuning every spare Unique into it. This is
   the first real investment decision in the plan and it comes before the
   weapon.
10. **Crafted Unique Dragon Lord Longsword and Guard**, or **Enraged Kromede**
    from Fire Temple Conquest at 2,400 if crafting is out of reach. Weapon to
    3 breakthroughs.
11. **Ferocious Horn Den (Nuakum)** and **Dying Dramata's Nest (Durvati)** to
    close any remaining armor gaps.
12. **Godstones**, now that the weapon and Guard are final.
13. **Every slot to +20 and 5 breakthroughs**, evenly, plus Belt and Amulet to
    Unique +10. This is the P6 finishing band.

## Manastones and Spirit Stones

Manastones go in weapons and armor; **Spirit Stones** go in accessories;
**Godstones** go only in the weapon and the Guard. Weapons and armor carry
five manastone sockets each at the top tier, and the weapon adds one Godstone
socket. Mechanics in [[Manastones Spirit Stones and Godstones]].

**Use Intermediate, not Superior.** A Superior manastone's blue-tier option
rolls 10 while an Intermediate's rolls 13, so three blue options on an
Intermediate reaches 39 against a Superior's typical 40 — for roughly a fifth
of the Kinah (about 960,000 against about 4,750,000 to reach 30 combined
Accuracy and Critical). Intermediate manastones need Alchemy craft level 20
and Intermediate spirit stones level 25, which is why Alchemy 25 is a Phase 1
task.

**Reroll breadth-first.** The Korean instruction is to accept a green-tier
option and move to the next slot rather than chase a yellow on one piece.
Raise the floor everywhere, then lift one slot at a time.

| Slot group | Primary | Then | Templar note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | **Accuracy** | Attack Power | Accuracy until you clear ~1,100, then Attack |
| Guard | **Accuracy** | **Block**, then Attack | The Guard is where Block manastones earn most, alongside its Block imprint line |
| Breastplate, Helm, Pauldrons, Gloves, Greaves, Boots, Cloak | **Accuracy** until the threshold | **Block**, then HP % or Damage Amplification | The generic tank answer is HP and Damage Tolerance here; the Templar answer prefers Block for Fury uptime |
| Rings, Earrings, Necklace, Bracelet (Spirit Stones) | **Attack %** | Accuracy, Critical | Spirit stone Attack % is the standard accessory line in every Korean guide |

Manastone reroll priority from [[Stat Reference]] is Damage Amplification
first, then Accuracy and Attack Power, then Critical and Guaranteed Critical.
**The Templar override is that Block enters at the same rank as Accuracy once
you are chasing the Fury threshold** — this is a class-specific deviation and
it is Korean-meta-derived, so re-check it once you can measure Fury uptime in
the live client.

**Not verified:** whether Block appears as a manastone option at all, or only
as a soul imprint and attribute-derived stat. No source I found lists the
full manastone option pool. If Block is not a manastone line, the armor
column above collapses to Accuracy then HP % and the entire Block budget has
to come from the Guard, Dexterity, Space, titles, wings and pet
understanding — which makes [[Templar Support Systems]] considerably more
important.

## Godstones (신석)

Weapon and Guard only, and the imprint is **destroyed on removal**, so this is
strictly Phase 5 work on final gear.

| Godstone | Effect | PvE | PvP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Extra Damage (추가 피해)** | extra damage on hit | ★★★★★ | ★★★ |
| **Defense Reduction (방어력 감소)** | lowers the target's Defense | ★★★★ | ★★★★ |
| Bleed (출혈) | damage over time | ★★★★ | ★★★ |
| Stun (기절) | stun on hit | ★★★ | ★★★★★ |
| Seal (봉인) | skill lock on hit | ★★ | ★★★★★ |
| Paralyze (마비) | full lockdown | — | ★★★★★, and very expensive |

**Season 1 PvE Templar: Extra Damage on the weapon, Defense Reduction on the
Guard.** That is the standard Korean split, and the reasoning is that Extra
Damage feeds your own numbers while Defense Reduction raises the *party's*
effective damage — which is exactly the trade a tank should want to make.

**Season 1 PvP Templar:** Stun on the weapon, Seal on the Guard. Paralyze is
better and is priced accordingly; the standing Korean advice is not to
enhance an expensive godstone onto a weapon you are not keeping.

Note that a Korean class-by-class godstone article recommending Blind
(실명) for a Guardian is **an AION 1 legacy article** and should not be used —
its class descriptions match the original game.

## Soul Imprint and attunement targets

Soul imprint unlocks at level 32 and should not be seriously used until past
item level 2,000, because rerolls consume the scarcest materials and are
wasted on gear you replace. Mechanics and reroll costs in
[[Soul Imprint and Soul Binding]].

**Tuning order** — from the Korean progression threads, and the Guard-first
ordering is the part that surprises people:

1. **Guard** at 2–3 breakthroughs
2. **Weapon** (crafted or dungeon)
3. **Gloves** and **Breastplate**
4. **Pauldrons** and **Boots**
5. **Ring**

**Per-slot targets.** The generic per-slot best lines are corroborated across
two independent Korean sources; the Templar-specific additions are flagged.

| Slot | Line 1 | Line 2 | Line 3 | Templar-specific |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weapon | **Weapon Damage Amplification %** | **Combat Speed %** | Might | Multi-hit chance |
| Guard | **Weapon Damage Amplification %** | **Combat Speed %** | **Block** (7% roll, 28–39 on Bakarma's Guard) | Might |
| Helm | Max HP % / Might | **Smite** | Attack increase | Smite is the Templar-relevant roll here |
| Pauldrons | **Critical Damage Amplification %** | Attack | — | — |
| Breastplate | **Damage Amplification %** | Block | HP % | Chest Damage Amplification is the single largest armor line in the game |
| Gloves | **Combat Speed %** | Attack | Critical | The only armor Combat Speed source |
| Greaves | **Block** | Dexterity | Attack / Critical | Dexterity converts into Evasion, **Block** and Critical Resist at 0.10%/point |
| Boots | Accuracy | Movement Speed % | Attack % | Accuracy while under the threshold |
| Cloak | **Class passive-skill level** | Smite | Attack increase | If it rolls a level on **Fury** or **Ironclad Defense**, take it over any stat |
| Ring | **Active skill level** (Mastery) | Attack % | — | This is how Judgment reaches 20 |
| Earring | **Passive skill level** | Attack % | — | Fury, Warding Shield, Ironclad Defense |

**The Korean consensus global order** across the whole character, for
reference when you are deciding which reroll to buy next: weapon Weapon
Damage Amp → weapon Combat Speed → Guard Weapon Damage Amp → Guard Combat
Speed → weapon Might → Guard Might → gloves Combat Speed → chest Damage Amp →
pauldrons Critical Damage Amp → weapon Multi-hit → Guard Multi-hit.

A Templar's honest deviation from that list is to insert **Guard Block** after
Guard Combat Speed, and to promote the **Cloak class-passive line** above
pauldrons whenever the roll is available, because a passive level on this
class is worth more than a percentage.

**Attunement / Soul Binding fodder.** Spare Unique drops are not vendor
trash. The Korean instruction is explicit: do not dismantle duplicate
Uniques, use them as tuning material, and funnel them into the Guard first,
then the weapon, then gloves and boots. This is the mechanism that turns a
mediocre drop rate into a finished Guard.

## Costs and math

- Decurion Abyss Point costs: rings 31,600 each; earrings, cloak, gloves
  42,100 each; necklace, boots, pauldrons 52,600 each; chest, legs, weapon
  60,000–120,000+. Medium confidence — one Korean blog transcribing shop
  prices.
- Abyss Point income: about 10,000 from the daily supply request, about 30,000
  from the weekly, about 10,000 per Abyss Corridor run while your faction
  holds an artifact, 2,500 from five daily missions, 200–300 per player kill.
- Enhancement targets: +5 to +8 while the story is the main activity, +10 once
  you are farming dungeons, +12 on a piece you are confident of keeping, +15
  as the practical weapon and Guard target, +20 plus breakthrough on final
  gear only. One Korean guide puts +15 as the risk boundary beyond which
  further breakthrough is a gamble.
- Breakthrough requires **Unique grade and +15**, and consumes roughly 900,000
  Kinah per step plus stones.
- Intermediate versus Superior manastones: about 960,000 Kinah against about
  4,750,000 for the same 30 combined Accuracy and Critical.
- Beyond Unique +10 on the Belt and Amulet, community reports describe tens of
  thousands of enhancement stones per level. Directionally right, no verified
  table.

## Traps and mistakes

- **Chasing a matched PvE armor set.** No PvE set has a registered set bonus.
- **Buying an expensive Decurion chest or weapon early.** They are the worst
  AP-per-point purchases; rings are the best.
- **Deep-enhancing one piece to hit a gate.** Same item level, several times
  the cost, and it empties the material buffer you need when the next tier
  drops.
- **Breaking through Vakron armor.** Transitional set. Breakthroughs go on the
  Guard, then the weapon, then accessories, then armor at 1–2 only.
- **Godstoning a stopgap weapon.** The imprint is destroyed on removal.
- **Soul imprinting a crafted item you meant to resell.** Imprinting makes it
  untradable permanently.
- **Dismantling duplicate Uniques.** They are your Guard's imprint budget.
- **Registering the source item in an equipment preset** before trying to use
  it as material. Preset-registered gear does not appear in the material list.
- **Assuming armor is class-restricted** and passing on a good piece.
- **Buying Superior manastones before the set is final.**

## Global launch notes

The whole ladder above assumes the Season 1 content roster: Krao Cave,
Draupnir, Urugugu Canyon, Fire Temple, Vakron's Sky Island, Ferocious Horn
Den, Dying Dramata's Nest, and the Rudra Sanctuary. NC has said dungeons and
PvP were reworked for the global release, so treat the *dungeon-to-item*
mapping as the most fragile part of this note and the *slot-to-stat* logic as
the durable part.

Specific things to check in the first week and record in
[[Global Launch Differences]]:

- Whether the Decurion set carries the −5% PvE Damage Boost penalty.
- Which Unique crafted Dragon Lord tiers appear in the Blacksmithing recipe
  list.

- Whether the 2025-12-31 armor and accessory enhancement doubling is applied.
- The shipped **item** English names, which will differ from several of the
  datamined names used above. Skill names in this note follow the live client;
  see [[Templar Skills and Stigmas]] and [[Glossary KR TW EN]].
